How the orrery is scaled and sourced

Scale

  • Orbital angles and positions are calculated from JPL Keplerian elements.
  • Planet and Sun sizes are scaled to the square root of their true radii.
  • Orbital distances use a logarithmic display scale to keep the inner orbits clear of the Sun.
  • Zoom changes every body through the same perspective projection. Zoom-out ends at the complete system view. Every planet retains its surface texture.
  • For example, Jupiter’s true radius is about 11 times Earth’s, but it is drawn about √11 ≈ 3.3 times larger.

Imagery

The default Sun maps Matteo’s surface detail onto a red-orange photosphere with limb darkening. Colour comparisons: golden highlights, white, amber, orange and red-orange. All use Matteo’s surface detail at the same exposure. The warmer colours are artistic options, not measured emission colours.

Custom Sun preview: a 3D sphere using NASA/SDO AIA observations from a full solar rotation. Credit NASA/SDO and the AIA science team. The surface combines observations from different dates. The orange colour and photographic prominence layers are illustrative. The layers approximate depth; they are not a measured 3D corona. No AI imagery is used.

Pedro’s complete model comparison uses High Resolution Solar system by Pedro B. Goulart (Pebegou), under CC BY 4.0. The preview adapts his meshes and materials to this orrery’s scale, lighting and rotation. The colours are artistic interpretations.

The Sun uses Matteo Pascale’s Realistic Sun scene, under the Blendkit Royalty Free licence. Its baked procedural detail is recoloured in the browser with reduced exposure. Custom animated plasma loops surround the Sun. They are illustrative, not a measured plasma simulation. His Earth scene remains an optional Earth preview, with adapted lighting and atmosphere. View original materials.

Earth: NASA/GSFC Blue Marble, with satellite cloud and polar imagery. Mars: NASA via NOAA Science On a Sphere. These are global image composites.

Original Sun comparison: an artist-prepared Solar System Scope global texture (CC BY 4.0), recoloured with the darker orange palette of the original SDO image. This is an illustrative reconstruction. The separate NASA/SDO prominence photograph rotates on a fixed plane. It does not represent a measured 3D corona. NASA imagery follows its media usage guidelines.

Uranus and Neptune: atmospheric reconstructions credited to NASA by ESO Supernova (Uranus, Neptune; CC BY 4.0). These are not direct global photographs.

Other textures and Saturn’s rings: Solar System Scope (CC BY 4.0). Milky Way backdrop: ESO / S. Brunier. Each body turns at its measured rotation rate from its IAU orientation, and planets are lit from the Sun’s direction; at fast time scales the spin is shown slowed so it stays readable.
