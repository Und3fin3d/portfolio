How a memristor stands in for a synapse

Ready. Regular inputs produce postsynaptic spikes after several inputs.

The pathway

Inside the device

Voltage redistributes the conductive state. A more continuous path gives higher conductance. The state relaxes after the voltage ends.

The sequence

Each step reads the output of the step before it. Nothing skips ahead.

Step 1 Presynaptic voltage

The spike travels the axon to the terminal. The electrode samples the complete voltage waveform at 20 kHz, one sample every 0.05 ms.

Step 2 Voltage across the device

Every sample is applied to the memristor. The device sees the whole waveform, not a spike time.

Step 3 Conductive state redistributes

The voltage moves the conductive state within the active layer. The conducting path improves and the device conductance rises.

Step 4 Postsynaptic current

The memristor outputs conductance. This illustration shows current magnitude: |I| = g |V - E_rev|. The code keeps the current sign.

Step 5 Receiving neuron may spike

The membrane voltage produces an action potential if it crosses the −36 mV firing threshold. The device state then relaxes.
